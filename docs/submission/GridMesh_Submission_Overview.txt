GridMesh: Federated Solar Forecasting for Feasible Neighbourhood Reserve

Neighbourhoods with growing rooftop and community solar face a practical reliability problem: renewable output can fall quickly when clouds arrive, while backup power is limited and expensive. Operators must avoid both under-scheduling, which risks unserved demand, and over-scheduling, which wastes energy and raises operating cost. Small solar owners also hold fragmented operational data and may be unwilling or unable to pool it. Sensor faults, missing clients and uneven data quality make a shared forecasting service harder to trust.

GridMesh is a decision-support prototype that connects privacy-conscious collaborative forecasting to a resource-constrained reserve schedule. Each solar site trains a 30-minute-ahead model locally and sends model updates rather than raw time-series histories. A coordinator combines those updates using data volume, data quality and a trust score that reduces the influence of unreliable clients. Event-aware participation lets stable sites skip some rounds while sites showing drift continue training. The repository implements MLP, GRU and LSTM alternatives, with the compact MLP selected by the current screening evidence.

At one aggregation node, GridMesh converts the combined forecast into an operational recommendation. Following the reserve-margin result in Khaing, Kannan and Rao (Clean Energy, 2026), it computes a causal n-sigma uncertainty margin from errors that would already be known at decision time. It also provides an empirical tail-quantile benchmark when the Gaussian assumption is doubtful. The margin is added to the expected demand-supply gap. A daily linear program then schedules grid import and backup subject to explicit power and energy limits. If the assets cannot cover the requirement, GridMesh shows a capacity warning rather than silently clipping the recommendation or claiming a false availability guarantee.

The implemented simulation uses real weather and irradiance, modelled PV output, simulated sites and faults, synthetic demand, and assumed asset and cost parameters. Across five seeds, the chosen n-sigma policy schedules 34.9% less backup, produces 10.3% less unserved energy and lowers assumed total cost by 17.2% versus a fixed 20%-of-demand uncertainty margin. Reliability-aware federated learning matches pooled training in the current simulation and limits healthy-site damage from two stale-sensor clients to 0.4%, versus 8.9% under standard FedAvg. At 100 simulated sites, event-aware participation reduces communication per round by 41% at similar forecast accuracy. Twenty-four automated checks cover leakage, causality, aggregation, faults, determinism and scheduler constraints.

GridMesh complements Schneider Electric’s broader DERMS and microgrid platforms. Its focused contribution is cross-owner federated forecasting, update reliability scoring and transparent forecast-to-reserve advice for smaller portfolios. The beneficiaries are households, local businesses, community energy operators and DISCOM teams. The next step is a shadow-mode pilot using measured multi-site PV and feeder demand, followed by secure aggregation, cybersecurity review and utility-approved integration.
