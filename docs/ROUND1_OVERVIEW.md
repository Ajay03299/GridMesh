# GridMesh: neighbourhood solar forecasting and reserve advice

Renewable electricity varies with weather, while neighbourhood demand continues. When solar output drops, a local operator must decide whether limited backup can cover the expected gap. Fixed reserve allowances can schedule unnecessary backup at some times and still leave shortfalls at others. Communities need understandable advice, explicit asset limits and a service whose complete cost can be examined.

GridMesh combines federated solar forecasting with uncertainty-aware, constrained backup recommendations. Separately managed sites train local models and share model updates rather than centralising raw histories. FedAvg combines these updates into a shared forecast. This architecture supports joint learning across independently managed sites, but the prototype does not demonstrate formal privacy or a real distributed deployment.

The operator workflow turns a +60-minute solar forecast, demand and grid estimates into an expected supply gap. A margin based on past observable forecast errors adds reserve for uncertainty. A causal, single-interval planner schedules backup within available power and the remaining daily scheduled-energy allowance. It displays any planned uncovered reserve rather than promising supply that assets cannot deliver. Realized unserved energy is evaluated separately using actual generation and delivered backup.

The implemented dashboard includes five reproducible synthetic scenarios: normal operation, cloud-driven shortfall, insufficient backup, stale or missing solar input and degraded-site/client-dropout status. Missing or stale solar triggers an explicitly labelled conservative planning bound and operator review. The operator confirms assets and approves or rejects advice outside the application. No equipment command is sent.

In a five-seed chronological Bengaluru simulation with 100 virtual sites, modeled photovoltaic generation and synthetic demand, the existing nominal policy increased fully supplied daytime intervals from 96.89% to 99.39%, reduced realized unserved energy from 0.875 to 0.128 MWh and reduced scheduled backup from 27.04 to 16.76 MWh against fixed 20% reserve. These are retrospective simulation outcomes, not measured outage hours. Selective updates reduced modeled protocol bytes 45.55% with a 2.72% relative forecast-error increase. Additional fault experiments did not establish consistent weighting benefits. Operating stresses expose important limits, including grid loss, unavailable backup and exhausted energy allowances.

A proposed 100-home pilot begins in shadow mode using existing assets, with qualified owner/operator oversight and a permission-based DISCOM information handoff. GridMesh proposes local forecast, uncertainty and reserve-advice signals alongside Schneider's existing grid-management capabilities; no partnership or integration is claimed.

An itemised cost model separates added service costs from existing energy and asset costs. Published hosting and Bengaluru connectivity rates combine with explicit paid-work, setup-recovery and contingency assumptions. Covered service costs are approximately 6,996–40,874 INR/month, with a 15,268 INR base estimate, excluding unquoted retrofit, additional asset access and energy-use changes. At 100 payers the base covered contribution is about 153 INR/month; at 50 it is 305 INR. These are cost-recovery estimates, not validated prices. Complete economics and willingness to pay remain unresolved. Intended beneficiaries are households, local operators and distribution utilities.
